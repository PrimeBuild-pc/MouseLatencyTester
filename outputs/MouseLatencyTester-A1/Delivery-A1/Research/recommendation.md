## Recommendation

Keep MouseLatencyTester and the dashboard, without archiving or deleting the repo. To build with the components already purchased, it is reasonable to keep the Teensy 2.0 in the first revision and develop the new optical front-end with local measurement of both edges. Confirmation depends on the real inventory of components and on verification of the firmware timer/interrupt. There is no evidence that a migration to Teensy 4.1, by itself, improves system accuracy.

The aim is a device inspired by the LDAT format, not a replica with demonstrated equivalent precision. The photograph provided is an aesthetic reference; it does not allow reliable dimensions to be derived.

## Measurements to distinguish

- Probe-to-PC: probe contact up to the software response; it also depends on the Windows/USB path and on calibration. It does not include the display.
- Probe-to-Photon: probe contact up to the detected light. In the current design it uses KY-018 and ADC; the new circuit must timestamp the comparator locally. The dashboard must not define the final instant of the optical measurement.
- USB click generated by the device: does not measure the latency of the physical mouse, which is not used.
- Display lag with a video generator: not equivalent to the complete mouse/PC/game/display path.

The external probe does not directly detect the electrical contact of the mouse switch. Mechanical alignment remains a source of error even with a faster optical sensor. Numerical resolution, USB rate, sensor speed and overall accuracy are different quantities.

## Two forms to evaluate, only one first production

1. **Compact, main proposal:** PCB and Teensy in the same case to be fixed to the monitor; BPV10 and analogue circuitry close to the optical window; buttons/connectors accessible; OLED possibly outward-facing or optional. Dimensions to be defined on the real components, not on the photo. The position must exclude ambient light and the light of any LEDs/OLED from the sensor path.
2. **Separate base and optical head:** small head with BPV10, TIA and comparator; base with Teensy, OLED and controls. Bring the digital signal out of the head and, if useful, a conditioned analogue output; avoid a long wire between photodiode and TIA input. More connectors and overall bulk, but less weight on the monitor and more convenient adjustments.

The first option was built as PCB A1. The separate head remains a possibility after bring-up, without a second production package. The purchased SOICs are soldered directly. The 3D case is not yet made: DXF with outline and holes included; assembly heights to be measured.

## Conditions for routing and exporting

Lorenzo confirmed the Teensy 2.0. The photos document BPV10, an additional BPW34, 3296W 10k, 15 pF NP0 0805, 100 nF radial, resistors with unreadable values, OPA380AIDR and TLV3501AID/AIDR SOIC-8, adapters and perfboards. The OLED is an SH1106 I²C 1.3 inch, VDD/GND/SCK/SDA; it is connected with a cable so as not to invent hole spacing. The 10 µF capacitors do not appear to have been purchased and Lorenzo authorises obtaining them: two ECEA1CKS100 selected, 10 µF 16 V radial Ø4 mm, height 5 mm, pitch 1.5 mm. See the [inventory](purchased-components.md).

Completed: schematic, layout with test points and trimmer access, ERC/DRC checks, schematic-PCB comparison and verification of the Gerbers/drill files from the same revision. To do: construction and stability/threshold tests, comparator firmware, timing verification and case. The assembly instructions and the review in the package distinguish these limits from the checks passed.

Rules compared with the [official PCBWay capabilities](https://www.pcbway.com/capabilities.html): two-layer FR-4, 1.6 mm, 1 oz, minimum tracks/spacing 0.2 mm, copper edge 0.5 mm, vias 0.7/0.3 mm. No quotes were prepared and no orders placed.

## Role of text-to-cad

The [public PCB skill](https://github.com/earthtojake/text-to-cad/blob/main/skills/pcb/SKILL.md) was used concretely for KiCad schematics/PCB, ERC/DRC and exports. The complete working copy is `hardware/compact/`; `hardware/optical-front-end/` keeps the first historical draft of the front-end only. The final package includes a KiCad 9 compatible copy, local libraries and licences. The conversion reported no losses and passes the checks. The tool helps the design but does not replace physical bring-up.
